# Internationalisation

> Governs the app's language on a phone or tablet and the direction of its layout.

## Locale

### app-languages-declared-to-the-system · SHOULD

The app's languages are declared to the system and chosen in it. The app declares the languages it ships as its localizations on iOS and its locale configuration on Android, and a language chosen on the device is the system's per-app language. A language picker inside the app sets the system's per-app language where the platform lets an app set it, as Android does, and otherwise, as on iOS, opens the app's page in the system's settings; the app stores no language choice of its own on the device beside the system's.

| Why |
|---|
| an app that declares no languages gets no per-app language in the system's settings, a choice the app stores on the device beside the system's is a second store of one choice, which disagrees with the first, and a person looks for the app's language where they set every other app's — a single source of truth. |

### locale-read-from-the-systems-per-app-language → locale-negotiated-from-the-user · MUST

On a phone, the language set for the app in the system's settings ranks first. It is its user's explicit choice; the account's setting follows, then the device's list as the platform's preferred languages; both are read at start and again when they change.

| Why |
|---|
| the per-app language is the person's explicit choice, made where they expect to make it, and an app that reads only the device's list overrides it. |

### layout-built-from-leading-and-trailing · SHOULD

A layout is built from leading and trailing edges, never left and right. The app declares its right-to-left support to the system, so the system mirrors it.

| Why |
|---|
| a layout built from left and right stays as it is in a right-to-left locale, and an app that declares no support is never mirrored. |
