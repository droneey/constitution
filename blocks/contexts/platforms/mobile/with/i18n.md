# Mobile with internationalisation

> Governs the app's language on a phone or tablet and the direction of its layout.

## Locale

### app-languages-declared-to-the-system · SHOULD
The app declares the languages it ships to the system — its localizations on iOS, its locale configuration on Android — so a person can choose the app's language in the system's settings.

| Why | Tags |
|---|---|
| a person then chooses the app's language where they choose every app's, and an app that declares none is missing from that list. | [ux] |

### locale-read-from-the-systems-per-app-language → locale-negotiated-from-the-user · MUST
On a phone, the language set for the app in the system's settings is its user's explicit choice, ranked first; the device's list follows as the platform's preferred languages; both are read at start and again when they change.

| Why | Tags |
|---|---|
| the per-app language is the person's explicit choice, made where they expect to make it, and an app that reads only the device's list overrides it. | [ux] |

### in-app-language-picker-sets-the-systems-choice · SHOULD
A language picker inside the app sets the system's per-app language, or opens the app's page in the system's settings, and keeps no choice of its own beside it.

| Why | Tags |
|---|---|
| two stores of one choice disagree, and a person who changes one sees the other win. | [ux] |

### layout-built-from-leading-and-trailing · SHOULD
A layout is built from leading and trailing edges, never left and right, and the app declares its right-to-left support to the system, so the system mirrors it.

| Why | Tags |
|---|---|
| a layout built from left and right stays as it is in a right-to-left locale, and an app that declares no support is never mirrored. | [a11y, ux] |
