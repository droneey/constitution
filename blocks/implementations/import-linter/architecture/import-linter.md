# import-linter

> The template's contracts hold core's layer matrix by the folders of its anatomy: what `kernel/`, `libs/`, `shared/`, `contracts/` and a feature's `domain/` may not import; features, adapters and entrypoints blind to each other; adapters kept from their callers; `root/` imported only by the entry files, and no entrypoint imported at all; `composition/` and the delivery layers `cli/` and `api/` imported by none of the layers below them; reads and writes apart; no layer folder's `__init__.py` imported; and the kernel, and a module of each layer from outside it, reached only through their `__init__.py`. A library's block keeps the library out of these folders with contracts of its own, in its section of the template, which `include_external_packages` lets name a package from outside. What no wildcard names — the delivery layer of a block the template does not know — the project adds as contracts of its own, or a reviewer checks.
