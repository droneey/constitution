# Accessibility

## wcag-aa-conformance · MUST
Every interface meets WCAG 2.2 AA — among its criteria, captions and transcripts, resize, reflow and text spacing, at most three flashes a second, a way past repeated blocks, a single-pointer alternative to dragging, a minimum target size, and consistent navigation.
**Why:** one measurable bar serves people and the tools that check for them, and AA is the bar law and practice expect.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y

## native-semantics-first · MUST
Native semantic elements come first; roles and states are added only where no native element exists, and only correct ones. A generic container never takes a press handler.
**Why:** a native element brings its role and keyboard for free; a wrong role is worse than none.
**Check:** tool — lint
**Tags:** a11y

## operable-by-every-input · MUST
Every interactive element works with every input the platform offers — keyboard, pointer, touch, assistive technology. Focus order follows the visual order, and nothing traps focus.
**Why:** a mouse is not the only way people use an interface, and one control that needs it locks them out.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y

## focus-always-visible · MUST
Focus is always visible, at 3:1 contrast against its background; no focus indicator is removed without the design system's replacement.
**Why:** a keyboard user who cannot see focus does not know where they are.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y

## every-control-has-an-accessible-name · MUST
Every control and field has an accessible name. A field has a visible label — a placeholder is only an example — and a hint where its format is not obvious; an icon-only control is named; a meaningful image has alternative text, and a decorative one is hidden.
**Why:** a screen reader announces a control by its name; without one, it says only "button".
**Check:** tool — lint
**Tags:** a11y

## status-changes-announced · MUST
Errors and changes of status are announced: an urgent one interrupts, the rest wait their turn. An invalid field is marked invalid and tied to its message.
**Why:** a change that is only shown is missed by everyone who cannot see it.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y, ux

## information-not-by-colour-alone · MUST
Colour never carries meaning alone; a status colour comes with an icon or a word.
**Why:** colour-blind users and monochrome screens lose whatever colour alone says.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y, ux

## contrast-tested-over-token-pairs · MUST
Contrast is proven by a test over every pair of semantic foreground and background tokens, in each theme: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text, and 3:1 for controls, borders and focus.
**Why:** a test over the token pairs proves every screen at once, and fails the change that breaks a pair.
**Check:** test
**Tags:** a11y, testing

## hover-content-reachable-by-focus-and-tap · MUST
Whatever hover reveals is reachable by focus and by a tap or long press; no action appears only on hover.
**Why:** touch screens have no hover, and keyboard users never produce one.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y, ux

## reduced-motion-honoured · MUST
When the user asks for reduced motion, motion that is not essential stops.
**Why:** motion makes some people ill; the setting is their request, and the interface keeps it.
**Check:** test
**Tags:** a11y

## headings-in-order · SHOULD
Headings go in order, and no level is skipped.
**Why:** screen reader users navigate by headings, and a skipped level reads as a missing section.
**Check:** test
**Tags:** a11y

## ui-specs-scan-accessibility · MUST
Every screen and component spec runs an accessibility scan, and passes with zero violations.
**Why:** a scan catches a third of the problems for free, on every change, before a person has to.
**Check:** test
**Tags:** a11y, testing
**Implements:** `spec-per-boundary`

## interactive-checked-by-hand · SHOULD
Before a new interactive component ships, a person operates it with the keyboard or a switch, and with a screen reader.
**Why:** tools see a third of the problems; the rest are found only by using the component as its users do.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y

## Requirements for implementation

What any accessibility scanner must provide.

## a11y-scan-inside-component-specs · MUST
The scanner runs over the rendered tree inside a spec, covers the WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules it can decide, and reports each violation with its element.
**Why:** without it, the scan cannot run in every spec, and a violation cannot be traced to its element.
**Check:** review
**Tags:** a11y, testing
